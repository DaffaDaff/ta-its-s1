\begin{abstract}
  Photo-based individual identification is an attractive non-invasive alternative to
  satellite tagging for sea turtle conservation, but published systems are typically
  reported as a single tuned configuration, leaving practitioners without guidance on
  which design choices actually matter. We fine-tune a DINOv3-pretrained Vision
  Transformer with an embedding head on SeaTurtleIDHeads under a strict open-set
  identity split, combining ArcFace and online hard triplet losses with layer-wise
  learning rate decay. We then vary nineteen parameters one factor at a time to build a
  sensitivity map. A conventionally chosen baseline reaches Rank-1 of $78.3\%$ and mAP of
  $84.9\%$; combining every individually-best setting reaches $90.4\%$ and $93.5\%$.
  Three findings are of practical interest. First, the backbone learning rate dominates:
  raising it alone from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$ yields Rank-1 of $87.8\%$, roughly $78\%$
  of the total gain, whereas six of the nineteen parameters move Rank-1 by under two
  points. Second, parameter effects are strongly sub-additive: summing the individual
  gains predicts $+33.7$ points, an arithmetically impossible figure, while only $+12.1$
  points materialize. Third, and most consequential for deployment, the embedding
  transfers to the unseen SeaTurtleID2022 domain at Rank-1 of $81.1\%$, yet open-set
  rejection of unenrolled individuals collapses the effective accuracy to
  $55$--$57\%$ at a $42\%$ false-reject rate. Two structurally different rejection rules
  converge on nearly identical operating points because all top-1 similarities occupy a
  narrow $0.85$--$0.98$ band with ROC-AUC of only $0.77$--$0.79$. The bottleneck is score
  calibration, not representation quality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% -------------------------------------------------------

Sea turtles are globally threatened, and effective conservation depends on tracking
individuals across time to estimate population size, migration routes, and
reproductive behaviour. The dominant instrumented approach, satellite tagging, is
expensive per unit, prone to device failure, and invasive enough to require careful
attachment to avoid harming the animal \cite{hays2018}.

Photo-identification offers a non-invasive alternative. The keratin scutes covering a
turtle's head form a geometric pattern that develops embryonically and does not
structurally regenerate, making it a stable natural biometric \cite{carpentier2016}.
Automated re-identification (Re-ID) over such patterns has progressed from hand-crafted
local descriptors \cite{gatto2018,dunbar2023} to convolutional networks
\cite{nguyen2023} and, most recently, to large self-supervised backbones
\cite{cermak2024}.

Re-ID is not closed-set classification. The model must map images to an embedding
space in which the same individual is close and different individuals are far apart,
and it must do so for identities never seen during training \cite{zheng2015}. This
open-set requirement is precisely what makes the task useful in the field, where new
animals are encountered continuously.

Existing sea turtle Re-ID work reports a single tuned system. That is sufficient to
establish feasibility, but it does not tell a practitioner adapting the pipeline to a
new site which knobs to turn first, nor how much of the reported accuracy survives a
change of camera and location. This paper addresses both gaps. Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A DINOv3 ViT Re-ID pipeline for loggerhead head images trained under an open-set
        identity split, evaluated with and without $k$-reciprocal re-ranking.
  \item A sensitivity map over nineteen parameters obtained by one-factor-at-a-time
        (OFAT) variation from a deliberately convention-based, not result-tuned, baseline.
        This ordering is, to our knowledge, the first published for animal Re-ID with ViTs.
  \item An explicit demonstration that OFAT gains do not compose. Summing individual
        improvements over-predicts the combined configuration by a factor of roughly $2.8$,
        and the predicted value exceeds $100\%$ Rank-1.
  \item A cross-dataset and open-set evaluation on SeaTurtleID2022 showing that
        representation quality transfers but rejection of unenrolled individuals does not, and
        localizing the failure to the similarity score distribution rather than to the
        embedding.
\end{itemize}

We deliberately report the negative open-set result in full. A conservation system that
silently rejects two of every five valid sightings is not deployable, and identifying
why is more useful than reporting only the favourable in-distribution number.

\section{Related Work}
% -------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Photo-Identification of Sea Turtles}

Carter et al.\ \cite{carter2014} applied neural network ensembles to green turtle
facial scutes, marking the shift from expert-dependent manual matching toward
reproducible automation. Carpentier et al.\ \cite{carpentier2016} validated facial
scute stability over an eleven-year span, supplying the biological justification for
head-based identity systems. Gatto et al.\ \cite{gatto2018} and Dunbar et al.\
\cite{dunbar2023} explored front-flipper scutes as an alternative region, with the
latter reporting higher accuracy for flippers under certain conditions, though head
images remain preferred in the field for ease of capture.

\subsection{Deep Learning Benchmarks}

Adam et al.\ \cite{adam2023} introduced SeaTurtleID2022, comprising 8{,}729 photographs
of 438 loggerhead individuals collected over thirteen years in the Greek islands, along
with a time-aware open-set protocol and a Hybrid Task Cascade plus ArcFace baseline
reaching Rank-1 of $86.8\%$. Nguyen et al.\ \cite{nguyen2023} benchmarked CNN
architectures on facial scutes, reaching $95.3\%$ with ResNet-50 on a mixed-quality set
of 1{,}426 images across 20 identities. \v{C}erm\'{a}k et al.\ \cite{cermak2024}
released the WildlifeDatasets toolkit and MegaDescriptor, a multi-species Re-ID
foundation model that outperforms generic pretrained backbones including CLIP and
DINOv2. Papafitsoros et al.\ \cite{papafitsoros2025} exploited visual similarity
between left and right facial profiles of the same individual, a signal conventional
photo-ID pipelines discard by partitioning libraries per side.

We emphasize that our numbers are not directly comparable to \cite{adam2023} or
\cite{nguyen2023}: the datasets, identity counts, and split protocols differ. Our
contribution is the relative structure of the design space, which is comparable across
setups in a way that absolute accuracy is not.

\subsection{Self-Supervised Vision Transformers}

The Vision Transformer \cite{dosovitskiy2021} processes an image as a sequence of
patches, allowing every patch to attend to every other at each layer. This suits Re-ID
of scute patterns, whose discriminative regions are spatially scattered across the
head. DINO \cite{caron2021} showed that self-distillation without labels induces
attention maps containing object boundary information at pixel resolution; DINOv2
\cite{oquab2024} scaled this to a curated 142M-image corpus with combined image- and
patch-level objectives; DINOv3 \cite{simeoni2025} further improves dense feature
correspondence and out-of-distribution generalization. Boundary-sensitive dense
features are directly relevant here, since no scute-level segmentation annotation
exists for turtles.

\subsection{Metric Learning Objectives}

ArcFace \cite{deng2019} applies an additive angular margin on the unit hypersphere,
giving a margin with consistent geodesic meaning regardless of class position. Triplet
loss \cite{schroff2015} with batch-hard mining \cite{hermans2017} optimizes relative
distances directly. Their linear combination is standard in competitive Re-ID, on the
premise that ArcFace provides global inter-class separation while triplet loss enforces
local distance consistency. One of our findings challenges that premise at this data
scale.

\section{Methodology}
% -------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Dataset and Open-Set Protocol}

We train on SeaTurtleIDHeads \cite{stidheads}, containing 7{,}582 head-cropped images
of 400 loggerhead (\textit{Caretta caretta}) individuals photographed over more than a
decade in Greek waters. The set exhibits wide variation in pose, illumination, viewing
angle, camera distance, and resolution, reflecting realistic field capture. We prefer
it over SeaTurtleID2022 as the training source because the latter has lower image
resolution and was designed primarily for segmentation.

The dataset is partitioned $70{:}20{:}10$ into train, validation, and test by
\emph{identity}, so no individual appears in more than one partition. All reported
evaluation is therefore open-set at the identity level. Identities with fewer than
$N_{\min} = N_G + 2$ images are discarded so that every retained identity can supply a
gallery and at least two queries. Validation and test partitions are each split into a
gallery of $N_G$ randomly chosen images per identity and a query set of the remainder.
Validation is used only for early stopping and checkpoint selection; test is touched
once.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Augmentation}

Images are resized to $\texttt{img\_size} \times \texttt{img\_size}$. Training
augmentation comprises random crop from a $+32$ pixel resize, horizontal flip
($p=0.5$), colour jitter over brightness, contrast, saturation and hue, random
grayscale ($p=0.1$), rotation up to $20^\circ$, Gaussian blur with a $5\times5$ kernel
and $\sigma \in [0.1, 2.0]$, and random erasing ($p=0.3$). Evaluation applies resize and
normalization only, keeping results deterministic. Pixel statistics follow ImageNet
conventions, matching the pretraining regime.

\subsection{Architecture}

The backbone is a DINOv3-pretrained ViT loaded from \texttt{timm}, with a $16\times16$
patch grid. At $256\times256$ input this yields $256$ patch tokens plus one class
token. The class token output of dimension $768$ (ViT-B) is projected by an embedding
head consisting of layer normalization, dropout with probability $p_{\text{drop}}$, and
a bias-free linear map to dimension $d_e$. The output is $L_2$-normalized, placing all
embeddings on the unit hypersphere so that cosine similarity reduces to a dot product.

\subsection{Objective}

ArcFace loss is the primary term:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{Arc}} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
  \log \frac{e^{s_a \cos(\theta_{y_i} + m_a)}}
  {e^{s_a \cos(\theta_{y_i} + m_a)} + \sum_{j \neq y_i} e^{s_a \cos\theta_j}}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_{y_i}$ is the angle between the sample embedding and its class prototype.
An online hard triplet term selects, for each anchor in the batch, the farthest positive
and nearest negative:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{Tri}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
  \max\!\big(d(a_i, p_i) - d(a_i, n_i) + m_t,\ 0\big)
\end{equation}
with masked selection to prevent zero-valued non-pair entries from being mistaken for
the smallest positive distance. The total objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Arc}} + \lambda_t \mathcal{L}_{\text{Tri}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Optimization}

Batches are formed by a balanced sampler drawing $P$ identities and $K$ images per
identity, guaranteeing valid positive pairs. The optimizer is AdamW with decoupled
weight decay $\lambda_w$. Layer-wise learning rate decay assigns transformer block $i$
of $L$ a rate
\begin{equation}
  \eta_i = \eta_{\text{bb}} \cdot \gamma^{\,L-i},
\end{equation}
so shallower blocks, which encode more generic features, are updated more conservatively.
The randomly initialized embedding head and ArcFace layer use a separate, higher base
rate $\eta_{\text{base}}$. A LambdaLR schedule applies linear warmup over $W$ epochs
followed by cosine decay to zero over the remaining $E - W$ epochs. Gradients are
clipped at $G_{\max}$ and mixed precision is enabled.

\subsection{Inference and Evaluation}

Embeddings are extracted in a single forward pass over the resized image and
$L_2$-normalized; no test-time augmentation is used, so the same protocol applies to
in-distribution and cross-dataset evaluation. Gallery images of one identity are
mean-pooled into a single prototype and renormalized. Similarity is computed as
$S = QG^\top$. We optionally apply $k$-reciprocal re-ranking \cite{zhong2017}, which
combines cosine and Jaccard distances,
\begin{equation}
  D_{\text{final}}(q,g) = \lambda_{rr} D_{\cos}(q,g) + (1-\lambda_{rr}) D_{\text{Jac}}(q,g),
\end{equation}
with $k_1 = 20$, $k_2 = 6$, $\lambda_{rr} = 0.3$, accelerated by FAISS. We report CMC at
ranks 1, 5, 10 and mAP.

\section{Experiments}
% -------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Experimental Setup}


Experiments run on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, Intel Core i9-13900KF, 64\,GB RAM, Python 3.10,
PyTorch 2.11 with CUDA 12.6, and \texttt{timm} 1.0.25. A fixed seed of 42 is set across
\texttt{random}, \texttt{numpy}, and \texttt{torch}.

The baseline in Table~\ref{tab:baseline_cfg} was fixed \emph{before} any variation
experiment, from conventions in the ViT and metric-learning literature adjusted for
dataset scale. This matters methodologically: had the reference point been chosen using
the variation results, the OFAT analysis would be circular and no parameter could be
shown to be mis-set. The conservative backbone learning rate that convention prescribes
turns out to be the single largest performance limiter, a finding only reachable
because the baseline was not tuned.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Baseline Performance}

Training loss decreases monotonically across all 50 epochs without spikes, indicating
that five warmup epochs and clipping at $1.0$ suffice for stability. Validation metrics
rise sharply during warmup and plateau under cosine decay with no late-epoch decline.
The absence of overfitting at $E=50$ is consistent with two later findings: extending
to $E=100$ still improves performance, and adding regularization degrades it. The model
is budget-limited, not regularization-limited.

Table~\ref{tab:baseline_res} shows a steep Rank-1 to Rank-5 gain of $14.0$ points
against only $2.8$ from Rank-5 to Rank-10. For roughly $14\%$ of queries the correct
identity is already within the top five but loses narrowly at rank one, so most failures
are ordering errors rather than retrieval failures. Qualitative inspection shows the
residual $4.9\%$ concentrated on blurred, low-contrast images where scute geometry is no
longer legible.

Re-ranking helps at the top of the list and on mAP but slightly hurts Rank-10, which is
expected: it optimizes neighbourhood consistency and occasionally pushes a correct
candidate out of a deeper cutoff. Net effect is positive but modest, on the order of one
point.

Three groups emerge.

\textbf{Sensitive but already correct.} $\eta_{\text{base}}$, $s_a$, and image size
degrade in either direction. The extreme case is $\eta_{\text{base}}$: a one-decade
shift either way costs more than $22$ points, since the randomly initialized head fails
to converge when the rate is too high and fails to learn within budget when too low.
These confirm the convention-based choices were right, and that getting them wrong is
expensive.

\textbf{Real headroom.} $\eta_{\text{bb}}$, $E$, $\lambda_t$, $p_{\text{drop}}$,
backbone, $m_a$, and $W$. Raising $\eta_{\text{bb}}$ to $10^{-4}$ alone gives Rank-1 of
$87.8\%$ and mAP of $91.6\%$. Because the trend is monotonic and $10^{-4}$ is the upper
bound tested, the true optimum may lie higher. Notably $\lambda_t = 0$ is best,
i.e.\ disabling triplet loss entirely gives Rank-1 of $83.4\%$ with performance
decreasing monotonically in $\lambda_t$. At this data scale ArcFace alone is
sufficiently discriminative and the triplet term interferes with optimization. This also
removes the justification for PK sampling, which explains why $K=2$ outperforms $K=4$.

Batch composition is better read through effective batch size than through $P$ or $K$
separately: both size-32 settings give identical Rank-1 of $79.1\%$ and both size-128
settings collapse to $68.5$--$71.9\%$. Under a fixed epoch budget, larger batches quarter
the number of update steps, so this is an under-training symptom, not a diversity effect.

\textbf{Negligible.} $d_e$, $m_t$, $G_{\max}$, $\gamma$, $P_{\text{stop}}$, $\lambda_w$
all move under two points, which on a query set of a few hundred amounts to a handful of
queries. With a single seed these cannot be distinguished from noise, so we claim
insensitivity rather than optimality for this group. The $P_{\text{stop}}$ result is
structural rather than empirical: at $E=50$ with evaluation every five epochs,
$P_{\text{stop}} \geq 10$ never fires.

\subsection{Combined Configuration and Sub-Additivity}

The combined configuration adopts every individually-best value from
Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} with positive $\Delta$: ViT-L/16, $p_{\text{drop}}=0$,
$m_a=1.0$, $\lambda_t=0$, $P=8$, $K=2$, $E=100$, $W=3$, $P_{\text{stop}}=20$,
$\lambda_w=10^{-5}$, $\eta_{\text{bb}}=10^{-4}$, $\gamma=0.65$; twelve changes in total.

    Combined                        & 89.1\%                              & 92.0\%                                & \textbf{90.4\%} & \textbf{93.5\%} \\

The combined configuration reaches Rank-1 of $90.4\%$ and mAP of $93.5\%$, gains of
$12.1$ and $8.6$ points. The interesting comparison is against the additive prediction.
Summing the $\Delta$ column gives $+33.7$ points, predicting $78.3 + 33.7 = 112.0\%$
Rank-1. The analogous mAP calculation predicts $105.6\%$. Both exceed the theoretical
ceiling, so additivity is not merely unsupported empirically but arithmetically
incoherent: only $21.7$ points of headroom exist while the sum demands $33.7$.

Realized gains are $36\%$ of predicted on Rank-1 and $42\%$ on mAP. The consistency of
this ratio across two metrics indicates a systematic property rather than a metric
artifact. Two overlap groups explain it. First, $\eta_{\text{bb}}$ ($+9.5$), $E$
($+6.1$), $W$ ($+2.7$), ViT-L ($+2.1$), and $\gamma$ ($+1.2$) all address the same
limitation, namely insufficient backbone adaptation to the turtle domain; once
$\eta_{\text{bb}}$ is raised, the deficit that $E=100$ would fix is largely already
resolved. Second, $\lambda_t$ ($+5.1$), $p_{\text{drop}}$ ($+2.7$), and $\lambda_w$
($+0.8$) are all relaxations of regularization on a model that was never overfitting,
so they harvest the same surplus. Several $\Delta$ values are below one point and
plausibly noise.

Critically, $\eta_{\text{bb}}=10^{-4}$ alone accounts for $9.5$ of the $12.1$ point
total, about $78\%$; the other eleven changes contribute $2.6$ points collectively.
Backbone learning rate tuning is not merely the most influential parameter but very
nearly the only one with substantial effect on this system.

The practical consequence is that concatenating OFAT optima does not guarantee an
optimum and can import harmful changes. One is already visible: $\gamma = 0.65$ was
selected on Rank-1 evidence ($+1.2$) despite reducing mAP by $0.2$ and Rank-5 by $2.1$
points in its own OFAT run. Verification by factorial design, or minimally by backward
ablation from the combined configuration, is required.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Generalization}

We apply the combined-configuration checkpoint to SeaTurtleID2022 \cite{adam2023}
without retraining. Twenty-five identities are drawn at random as registered, with
$N_G=3$ gallery images each and the remainder as queries; images are head-cropped to
match training preprocessing. Since the inference protocol is identical to the
in-distribution evaluation, any degradation is attributable to domain shift alone.

transfer carries a measurable cost, but it is not catastrophic. Rank-5 of $97.2\%$ in
fact exceeds the in-distribution combined Rank-5 of $96.6\%$, and cross-dataset Rank-1
still exceeds the in-distribution \emph{baseline}. The fine-tuned DINOv3 embedding does
generalize across capture conditions. t-SNE projections show clusters that remain
separable but are looser intra-class and closer inter-class than in-distribution,
consistent with the observed drop.

\subsection{Open-Set Rejection}

Field deployment must handle photographs of animals never enrolled. Adding five
unregistered identities brings the query set to 266, comprising 217 registered and 49
unregistered. We compare two rejection rules on the identical query set.

\textbf{Global threshold (absolute).} The top-1 cosine score is compared against a
single threshold tuned by maximizing Youden's $J$.

\textbf{Background class (relative).} A set of background prototypes drawn from turtle
images outside the gallery, kept as multiple prototypes rather than pooled, yields a
relative score $s = s_{\text{reg}} - s_{\text{bg}}$, where $s_{\text{bg}}$ is the maximum
similarity to any background prototype. A query is accepted if $s \geq \text{margin}$,
with the margin tuned under a bounded false-reject constraint. Recomputing
$s_{\text{bg}}$ per query is intended to normalize out hub identities.

Registered Rank-1 falls from $81.1\%$ without rejection to $55.3\%$ and $56.7\%$
respectively. The ranking model is unchanged between these settings; only a rejection
stage placed before ranking has been added. The source is the false-reject rate of
roughly $42\%$: nearly half of genuinely enrolled queries are discarded before they can
be ranked.

The important observation is not which rule wins but how little separates them. Despite
mechanistically opposite designs, absolute versus relative, they converge on nearly the
same operating point, with all differences on the order of one or two queries. Among
accepted registered queries, retrieval accuracy is $0.567/(1-0.419) \approx 97.6\%$ for
the background scheme and $\approx 97.7\%$ for the threshold, confirming that both
rules simply discard the hard cases first.

The cause lies in the score distribution. Every top-1 similarity, registered or not,
falls in a narrow band of approximately $0.85$ to $0.98$. An image of a turtle absent
from the gallery still scores about $0.90$ against some other individual's prototype,
because all images share nearly identical structure, a turtle head against an underwater
background, placing embeddings in a tight cone on the hypersphere where any two images
are already similar before identity is considered. The unregistered distribution is not
merely shifted but embedded within the lower tail of the registered distribution, so any
threshold high enough to reject all unregistered queries necessarily rejects most
registered ones. The $43.4\%$ false-reject rate is a direct consequence of this
geometry, not of poor threshold tuning; the Youden optimum is a corner solution chosen
because no better compromise exists.

For the background scheme, plotting $s_{\text{reg}}$ against $s_{\text{bg}}$ shows the
two classes separating along the horizontal axis only. On the vertical axis they overlap
almost completely, and $s_{\text{bg}}$ shows no strong dependence on $s_{\text{reg}}$.
The relative score is therefore effectively $s_{\text{reg}}$ plus noise, which is why it
reaches the same operating point. Overall separability is only moderate, with ROC-AUC of
$0.77$ to $0.79$, and this bounds every possible decision rule over these scores.

Qualitative inspection is decisive. In the five most confidently wrong decisions, all
five are registered identities rejected as unknown, none are false accepts, and in every
case the correct identity remains within the top five candidates, at rank one for three
of them with scores of $0.853$, $0.867$, and $0.868$. Meanwhile a correctly rejected
unregistered query scores $0.851$. A correct match and a completely unknown animal
produce practically indistinguishable scores. Correct confident decisions, by contrast,
score $0.966$ to $0.976$ with a clear gap to the runner-up. The system decides reliably
only when a similarly-posed gallery image pushes the score above roughly $0.95$;
otherwise everything concentrates in the same $0.84$ to $0.88$ band.

We caution that the $100\%$ unregistered recall is obtained from only 49 unregistered
queries with a threshold tuned on the same set, and must not be read as a guarantee of
perfect rejection on new data.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}
% -------------------------------------------------------

Three implications follow.

\textbf{Tune the backbone learning rate first.} For ViT-based animal Re-ID, the standard
conservative fine-tuning rate of $10^{-5}$ substantially under-adapts a self-supervised
backbone to a fine-grained wildlife domain. Practitioners with a limited compute budget
should sweep $\eta_{\text{bb}}$ before touching loss hyperparameters, whose combined
effect here is an order of magnitude smaller.

\textbf{Reconsider the ArcFace-plus-triplet default.} The combination is near-universal
in Re-ID, but at a few hundred training identities the triplet term monotonically
degraded performance. Its benefit likely requires either more identities or a backbone
that has not already been pretrained toward strong local discrimination.

\textbf{Open-set rejection is the deployment bottleneck.} The representation transfers;
the decision rule does not. Because failure is in score calibration rather than
embedding quality, the productive directions are calibration and score normalization,
for instance distance normalization against a cohort, learned rejection heads, or
explicit training-time exposure to unenrolled individuals, rather than further backbone
scaling. Rejection thresholds must additionally be tuned on a held-out set, which our
protocol did not do.

The main methodological limitation is single-seed evaluation. Parameters with ranges
below two points cannot be separated from run-to-run variance, and the exact match
between two configurations across three metrics observed for $\gamma=0.85$ warrants
verification against experiment logs. OFAT also evaluates each parameter at an operating
point that Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} shows to be under-trained, so some measured
effects are conditioned on a non-converged reference.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented a DINOv3 ViT sea turtle Re-ID system evaluated under open-set identity
splits, together with a sensitivity map over nineteen parameters. A convention-based
baseline reaches Rank-1 of $78.3\%$ and mAP of $84.9\%$; combining individually-best
settings reaches $90.4\%$ and $93.5\%$, with $k$-reciprocal re-ranking contributing
about one point in both cases. Backbone learning rate is the dominant factor,
contributing roughly $78\%$ of the total improvement on its own, while six parameters
move Rank-1 by under two points. Parameter effects are strongly sub-additive: only
$36\%$ of the additively predicted gain materializes, and the additive prediction
exceeds $100\%$, so OFAT should be used to order parameters by importance, never to
forecast combined performance.

The embedding transfers to an unseen capture domain at Rank-1 of $81.1\%$ and Rank-5 of
$97.2\%$ without retraining. Open-set rejection, however, reduces effective accuracy to
$55$--$57\%$ at a false-reject rate near $42\%$, and two structurally different rejection
rules converge on nearly identical operating points because top-1 similarities occupy a
narrow band with ROC-AUC of only $0.77$--$0.79$. Since correct identities remain in the
top five even in the worst failures, the limitation is calibration, not representation.
Future work should pursue multi-seed evaluation with factorial verification, held-out
threshold tuning with more unenrolled identities, exploitation of left-right profile
similarity, integration of head detection into an end-to-end pipeline, and validation on
species beyond loggerhead.
